Before the visit: what changed, and what might need a second look

Jane Natividad  ·  jane-natividad.github.io  ·  View on GitHub

Patients managing an ongoing condition can fall through the gaps between visits: a dose changed by a specialist that never matched what the pharmacy dispensed, a referral sent and never closed, a lab that's quietly overdue. This prototype reads a patient's record between visits and prepares the physician before they walk in — what's changed since last time, what may need a second look, and questions worth asking. It supports clinical review. It doesn't diagnose or recommend treatment, and every flag traces back to the record entry behind it.

Questions worth asking
    How the flags are made
    1
    Rules, not a modelEvery flag comes from a plain, readable rule: prescribed vs. dispensed dose, monitoring intervals, orders with no result, abnormal results with no follow-up. Same record in, same flags out.
    2
    Every flag cites its sourceEach one shows the record entry IDs it was built from, so a physician can check it in seconds rather than trust it.
    3
    The physician closes the loopFlags can be marked reviewed or not relevant. The tool never marks anything resolved by itself and never suggests a diagnosis or treatment.
    • No real patient data — all three patients are synthetic, and nothing you click leaves this browser tab.
    • No connection to an EMR, pharmacy, or lab system. A real version would need integration, privacy review, and clinical validation.
    • Monitoring intervals are simplified demonstration values, not clinical guidance.
    "Physicians don't need another alert. They need the three things that changed since the last visit, already pulled together, with the receipts attached."
    Demonstration prototype with synthetic patient data. For illustration of clinical-review support only; not a medical device and not medical advice.
